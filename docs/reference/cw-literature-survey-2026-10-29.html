Every way the field has tried to pick the contention window for 802.11 and other CSMA/CA systems: standard rules, analytical optima, heuristics, feedback control, game theory, supervised learning, bandits, deep and multi-agent RL, and the 2024 to 2026 work on LLMs, transformers and Wi-Fi 7 and 8. Bottom line for mac-world

  1. The closest published work is drlCW (Yan et al., VTC 2024-Spring). It is per-AP multi-agent DDQN for 802.11bn with neighbour observations shared through beacons, and a reward that is positive only when an AP is no worse than CSMA on throughput and loss. That gate is a direct fix for the selfish CW race the earlier report diagnosed (D1). It must be cited and is the best template for our next reward.
  2. Strong non-learning baselines exist and one paper shows RL failing to beat them. Kosek-Szott et al. (2021) found DQN gave no meaningful gain over a tuned heuristic for 802.11ax UORA. A reviewer will ask for IdleSense, the Patras PI controller, or the 2026 BLADE rule, not only BEB.
  3. Gawłowicz, Walrand and Wolisz (2021) is a ready-made multi-agent baseline in our exact stack. ns-3 plus ns3-gym, per-node CW, decentralised gradient search for proportional fairness, about +20 % over BEB in one collision domain, and a finding that purely local utility oscillates.
  4. Hardware evidence supports a pinned CW. Learning to Wait (CableLabs, 8 real APs) shows a well-chosen CWmin = CWmax beats BEB by up to 155 % median throughput, with Jain 0.98 against 0.82. This answers the obvious reviewer worry about OSCAR-style pinning.
  5. Most single-AP DRL papers report +30 % to +45 % over BEB on custom 802.11b-like simulators. Few of them compare against the best static CW, so part of that gain is likely just the static-optimum gap (inference), not proof the learner is smart. This backs the earlier report's ceiling sweep (#1).
  6. The 2025 to 2026 frontier is constrained MARL (FC-MAPPO, QaSAL), transformer in-context learning that adapts to unseen station counts without retraining (MobiHoc 2025), LLM agents for multi-AP coordination, differentiable digital twins for fast pre-training, and standard changes in 802.11bn (P-EDCA, NPCA, MAPC) that make CW behaviour conditional and coordinated.
Reported gains are rarely comparable. Papers differ in PHY (802.11b at 11 Mbps up to 802.11ax HE-MCS11), simulator (ns-3, ns-2, MATLAB, custom Python, WiPySim, testbeds), traffic (mostly saturated), and baseline (BEB, CCOD, or a static setting). Many recent DRL papers have no BEB baseline at all. Compare methods inside mac-world, on paired seeds.

How the field developed

1994–1999
Backoff rules are invented.

MACAW's MILD, then the 802.11 DCF with binary exponential backoff. CW is a fixed rule: double on failure, reset on success.

2000–2005
The optimum becomes computable.

Bianchi's Markov model and Calì, Conti and Gregori's p-persistent analysis show the optimal CW grows with the number of stations and that BEB is far from it at high load. Gentle-decrease rules (SD, DIDD, EIED, GDCF), FCR and AEDCF tweak BEB. EDCA standardises per-class CWs.

2003–2011
Estimate, then control.

Kalman and Bayesian estimators of the number of contenders, AOB's slot-utilisation filter, Idle Sense's idle-slot AIMD, and the Patras PI controller with a hardware testbed. These reach near-optimal throughput without learning.

2005–2011
Selfishness and optimality theory.

Game theory shows selfish CW choice is inefficient (Čagalj, Konorski) and designs utility-driven MACs with good equilibria (random access game). Adaptive CSMA proves queue-based access is throughput-optimal in any conflict graph. Proportional-fair CWs get a closed form.

2008–2016
Collision-free and dense-network ideas.

Deterministic backoff after success (L-BEB, CSMA/ECA) and 802.11ax-era dense-network proposals.

2015–2020
Learning arrives.

Post-decision-state Q-learning, Q-learning over COSB (iQRA), fuzzy and supervised CW predictors, DLMA's DRL multiple access, load-based CW prediction on real APs, ns3-gym.

2021–2023
Deep RL for one AP.

CCOD (DQN, DDPG), SETL-DQN, DDQN variants and OSCAR: a central agent at the AP sets one CW for all its stations from a collision or loss history.

2024–2026
Many agents, constraints and new models.

Per-AP MARL with neighbour observations (drlCW), federated DRL, constrained MAPPO, bandits that pick channel and CW together, Gumbel exploration, LSTM-SAC for Wi-Fi 7 MLO, transformer in-context learning, LLM-driven MAC design, differentiable twins, and 802.11bn's P-EDCA, NPCA and multi-AP coordination.

Standards and protocol mechanisms 8
1997/99
DCF with binary exponential backoff (BEB)
IEEE 802.11-1997, 802.11a/b
in earlier reportknown
 Draw backoff uniformly in [0, CW]; CW doubles after each failed attempt from CWmin to CWmax, resets to CWmin on success or after the retry limit. The standard every paper compares against. ns-3 default for 802.11ax BE: CWmin 15, CWmax 1023. Reference. Collision rate rises fast with N (FC-MAPPO measures about 40 % at 10 saturated STAs in ns-3).

For mac-world: Our BEB baseline is ns-3 dryRun, CW 16..1024.

 High
2005
EDCA (802.11e)
IEEE 802.11e
newknown
 Four access categories, each with its own CWmin, CWmax, AIFSN and TXOP limit. The AP advertises parameters in beacons (EDCA Parameter Set). Basis of every QoS-aware CW paper. Prioritises voice and video by giving them smaller CW and AIFS.

For mac-world: Beacon-advertised EDCA parameters are the real-world channel by which an AP can push a CW to its STAs and neighbours can read it.

 Medium
2021
MU EDCA and UORA (802.11ax)
IEEE 802.11ax
newknown
 MU EDCA: separate, usually larger EDCA parameters for STAs after they are served by trigger-based uplink. UORA: OFDMA random access with its own OFDMA backoff (OBO) and OCW from 7 to 31. Wi-Fi 6 MAC. Moves part of contention into AP-scheduled OFDMA. Low
2016+
LAA / NR-U Cat-4 LBT contention window adjustment
3GPP Rel-13 LAA, Rel-16 NR-U
newknown
 Cat-4 listen-before-talk with priority classes. The CW is raised when a large share of HARQ feedback in a reference slot is NACK, and reset otherwise. Licensed-assisted access in 5 GHz, coexisting with Wi-Fi. Feedback-driven CW, not per-frame BEB. Many DRL coexistence papers tune this CW. Low
2024
MLO channel access (802.11be, Wi-Fi 7)
IEEE 802.11be
newknown
 Multi-link devices contend on several links (STR, NSTR, EMLSR). Each link has its own backoff. Wi-Fi 7. Adds a new decision: which link to contend on, and with what CW. Low
2025-26
P-EDCA (802.11bn, Wi-Fi 8)
IEEE 802.11bn drafts; Liu et al., arXiv 2607.19931
newread
 Prioritised EDCA for latency-sensitive voice traffic. Triggered when the AC_VO retry counter hits a threshold: the STA sends a DS-CTS that suspends other backoffs, then contends with a small fixed CW (reported CWmin = CWmax = 7, AIFSN 2) and protects its TXOP with RTS/CTS. Single BSS, 80 MHz, 10 STAs (5 using P-EDCA), and an OBSS case. Latency reduced by more than 40 % beyond 5 Mbps load in a single BSS, and more than 30 % in OBSS (paper's own simulation).

For mac-world: Shows the standard itself is moving toward conditional, state-triggered CW changes. Note that P-EDCA pins CWmin = CWmax, like OSCAR.

 Medium
2025-26
NPCA, MAPC (Co-TDMA, Co-SR, Co-BF, Co-RTWT) in 802.11bn
IEEE 802.11bn; Liu et al. 2026; Nunez et al. 2026
newread
 NPCA: switch to a non-primary channel when the primary is busy and run backoff there. MAPC: APs coordinate who transmits in a TXOP instead of each contending blindly. Multi-AP Wi-Fi 8. NPCA: +16 % to +36 % throughput and 49 % to 72 % lower downlink latency in the paper's two-BSS case. MAPC DRL scheduling: up to 30 % lower worst-case delay than the best heuristic.

For mac-world: Multi-AP coordination is where Wi-Fi 8 is heading. A per-AP CW agent with neighbour information is a light form of it.

 Medium
2022-23
IEEE 802.11 AIML TIG: distributed channel access use case
IEEE 802.11 AIML Topic Interest Group technical report
newfound
 The standards group lists ML-tuned distributed channel access (including CW) as a candidate use case, alongside spatial reuse, roaming and rate control. Technical report text, not an algorithm. Confirms ML-based CW tuning is on the standards radar for Wi-Fi 8 and later.

For mac-world: Useful framing for the report's introduction.

Analytical models 6
2000
Bianchi Markov model of DCF
G. Bianchi, IEEE JSAC 18(3)
in earlier reportknown
 2-D Markov chain of backoff stage and counter. Fixed point gives attempt probability tau and conditional collision probability p for n saturated stations, then throughput. Single collision domain, saturated, ideal channel. Optimal CW grows roughly linearly with n, about n·sqrt(2·Tc/σ) (Bianchi; Bianchi and Tinnirello).

For mac-world: Already in earlier report. Use it to compute CW* for our timings as a model-based baseline.

 High
2000
p-persistent approximation and theoretical throughput limit
F. Calì, M. Conti, E. Gregori, IEEE/ACM ToN
newknown
 Replace BEB by a p-persistent protocol with the same mean backoff. Derive the p (equivalently CW) that maximises throughput, and estimate the number of stations at run time to tune it. Single collision domain, saturated. Showed standard 802.11 can be far from the capacity limit and that run-time tuning closes most of the gap.

For mac-world: The first 'dynamic tuning' paper. Same idea as OSCAR without learning.

 Medium
2002-07
Model refinements: retry limits, fixed points, heterogeneity
Wu et al. INFOCOM 2002; Kumar, Altman, Miorandi, Goyal ToN 2007; Ziouva and Antonakopoulos 2002
newknown
 Add retry limit, post-backoff, freezing, and non-saturated traffic. Kumar et al. show the fixed point is unique and that throughput depends mainly on the attempt rate. Analytical. Basis for the claim that in one collision domain only the aggregate attempt rate matters.

For mac-world: Supports the earlier report's ceiling argument: per-AP CWs mostly redistribute airtime in one collision domain.

 Medium
2003
Performance anomaly
Heusse, Rousseau, Berger-Sabbatel, Duda, INFOCOM 2003
newknown
 Shows DCF gives equal transmission opportunities, so one slow station drags every station's throughput down to near its rate. 802.11b multi-rate. Motivated airtime fairness and Idle Sense. Low
2025
Analytical MLO EDCA + genetic algorithm
Yi, Cheng, Wang, Pan, Ouyang, Zhang, arXiv 2509.25855
newread
 AIFS-zone fixed-point model for multi-link EDCA gives loss and delay CCDF. A genetic algorithm searches CWmin, CWmax, AIFSN, TXOP and retry limit per AC per link. 5-AC BSS on 2 STR links, no named simulator. Qualitative only (figures): optimised MLO EDCA beats optimised single link and default settings. Low
2026
Bianchi model extended to MLO + LSTM-SAC
Z. Liu et al., arXiv 2603.18602
newread
 Extends the Bianchi chain to Wi-Fi 7 STR MLO. An LSTM-SAC agent jointly picks the traffic split across links and each link's initial CW, from a history of SNR and channel busy time. MATLAB event simulator driven from Python/Gym, 1 AP, 10 STAs, 2 links. +6 % throughput over plain SAC and +37 % over fixed initial CW (summary-level figures, no BEB baseline).

For mac-world: Uses channel busy time as input, which supports our planned idle/busy observation.

Heuristic backoff rules 7
1994
MILD (MACAW)
Bharghavan, Demers, Shenker, Zhang, SIGCOMM 1994
newknown
 Multiplicative increase (×1.5) on collision, linear decrease (−1) on success, plus copying the backoff value heard in packet headers. Indoor wireless LAN, pre-802.11. Fairer than BEB and avoids the sharp reset to CWmin.

For mac-world: Backoff copying is an early form of neighbours sharing their CW.

 Medium
2003-05
Gentle decrease family: SD, DIDD, EIED, LMILD, GDCF
Ni, Aad, Barakat, Turletti (slow decrease) 2003; Song et al. (EIED) VTC 2003; Chatzimisios et al. (DIDD); Wang, Li, Li (GDCF) IEEE TVT 2004
newknown
 After a success, do not reset CW to CWmin. Halve it (SD, DIDD), divide by a factor (EIED), or reset only after c consecutive successes (GDCF). Analytical plus ns-2. Gains of roughly 10 % to 30 % throughput over BEB at high load are typical in these papers, because CW stays near the right size.

For mac-world: A cheap baseline that keeps BEB's safety net. Relevant to idea #8 (agent sets CWmin, backoff rule kept).

 Medium
2003
Fast Collision Resolution (FCR)
Kwon, Fang, Latchman, INFOCOM 2003
newknown
 Small CWmin, larger CW growth on collision, and stations that sense a busy slot increase their backoff, so a winner tends to keep winning briefly. Analytical plus simulation. Higher throughput than BEB at moderate load; fairness needs extra fixes. Low
2003
AEDCF (adaptive EDCF)
Romdhani, Ni, Turletti, WCNC 2003
newknown
 Per-AC CW update based on an estimate of the collision rate, with a slower decrease after success. ns-2, 802.11e. Reported large gains in medium utilisation over fixed EDCA. Low
2008-14
Deterministic backoff: Learning-BEB, CSMA/ECA, CSMA/E2CA
Barcelo, Bellalta, Cano, Oliver 2008; Sanabria-Russo et al. arXiv 1412.1395
newfound
 After a successful transmission, a station uses a fixed (deterministic) backoff next time. Stations converge to a collision-free schedule. Hysteresis and fair-share variants handle density. Simulation, dense WLANs. Approaches collision-free operation in saturated single-collision-domain networks.

For mac-world: A non-RL scheme that targets exactly the single-collision-domain setting our simulation has today.

 Medium
2016
802.11ax dense-network CW proposals (EICW, TDuCSMA, CSMA/ECA)
da Silva, Nogueira, arXiv 1611.06609
newread
 Survey plus small ns-3 study of collision avoidance proposals for dense 802.11ax. ns-3, saturated UDP. All cut collisions versus 802.11n DCF, but lower collisions did not always mean higher throughput.

For mac-world: Reminder to report throughput, not only collision rate.

 Low
2026
BLADE (HIMD contention control)
Guo, Zhou, ..., Xie, Liu (Tencent, PKU and others), arXiv 2603.16119
newread
 Hybrid increase / multiplicative decrease of a shared 'microscopic access rate' signal. Every transmitter runs the same local rule. Aimed at tail latency for real-time traffic. ns-3 802.11ax (2 to 16 AP-STA pairs), commodity APs, 24-room apartment, game and video traces. Tail latency more than 5× lower and video stalls more than 90 % lower than 802.11 BEB under heavy contention. Apartment p99.99 latency 120 ms against more than 500 ms.

For mac-world: A 2026, rule-based, distributed multi-AP scheme with a real deployment. Strong candidate for a non-RL baseline beside IdleSense, and evidence that tail latency is a metric where per-AP control wins.

Estimation and feedback control 6
2003
Kalman estimation of the number of contenders
Bianchi, Tinnirello, INFOCOM 2003
in earlier reportknown
 Estimate n from the measured conditional collision probability, n ≈ 1 + log(1−p)/log(1−τ), smoothed by an extended Kalman filter. Then set CW*. Analytical plus simulation. Tracks n accurately and quickly.

For mac-world: Already in earlier report.

 High
2004
Asymptotically Optimal Backoff (AOB)
Bononi, Conti, Gregori, IEEE TPDS 2004
newknown
 Measure slot utilisation and add a probability of skipping a transmission, so the effective attempt rate stays near optimal. Works on top of standard backoff. Simulation. Close to the theoretical throughput limit and robust to load changes.

For mac-world: A 'filter' on top of BEB, an alternative to replacing BEB.

 Medium

Idle Sense
Heusse, Rousseau, Guillier, Duda, SIGCOMM 2005
in earlier reportknown
 Every station measures the mean number of idle slots between transmissions and adjusts CW by AIMD toward a target (about 5.68 for 802.11b). Gives time fairness as well. ns-2 and analysis. Near-optimal throughput and fairness, fully distributed.

For mac-world: Already in earlier report as the key non-RL baseline.

Bayesian estimation of competing stations
Toledo, Vercauteren, Wang, IEEE TMC 2006
newknown
 Sequential Monte Carlo (particle filter) estimate of n, then optimal CW. Simulation. Faster tracking than Kalman under abrupt changes. Low
2009-11
Control-theoretic CW tuning (PI controller)
Patras, Banchs, Serrano, Azcorra, IEEE TMC 2011; testbed paper arXiv 1203.2970
newfound
 A PI controller drives the measured conditional collision probability to the analytically optimal target. Distributed and centralised versions. Stability proven with control theory. Simulation plus a real testbed on commodity hardware. Near-optimal throughput, stable, fast convergence; confirmed on hardware.

For mac-world: Very close to what our per-AP agent tries to learn: a feedback loop on collision probability. Cheap, explainable baseline with stability guarantees.

 High
2019
Learning to Wait (load-based CW prediction)
Sandholm, Huberman, Hamzeh, Clearwater (CableLabs), arXiv 1912.06747
newread
 Predict the optimal fixed CW (CWmin = CWmax, BEB off) from active transmitters and last throughput, with linear regression, naive Bayes or a small DNN. One-step prediction, not RL. Real testbed: 8 OpenWrt 802.11ac APs, 80 MHz, hostapd patch, traffic traces. Median throughput up to +155 % with 8 transmitters over default BEB; online average +26 % (DNN). Jain 0.98 with the optimal fixed CW against 0.82 for BEB.

For mac-world: Hardware evidence that a well-chosen pinned CW (as in OSCAR) beats BEB in a multi-AP setting, and that a simple regressor can find it.

Optimal CSMA theory and fairness 4
2008-10
Adaptive CSMA (throughput- and utility-optimal)
Jiang, Walrand, IEEE/ACM ToN 2010
newfound
 Each link adjusts its access aggressiveness from its own queue length or a virtual queue. Proven throughput-optimal and utility-maximising in general conflict graphs. Theory plus simulation, arbitrary interference graphs. Achieves the capacity region with only local information (in the idealised model).

For mac-world: The theoretical answer to 'per-node CW in a partial-overlap topology'. Matters once idea #7 (partial overlap) is built.

Q-CSMA and utility-optimal CSMA
Ni, Tan, Srikant, INFOCOM 2010; Liu, Yi, Proutière, Chiang, Poor 2010
newknown
 Discrete-time, collision-aware versions of adaptive CSMA with queue-based access probabilities. Theory plus simulation. Throughput-optimal with collisions taken into account. Low
2011
Proportional fairness in 802.11 WLANs
Checco, Leith, IEEE Comm. Letters 2011
newknown
 Derives the per-station CW (attempt rates) that maximise the sum of log throughputs. The PF allocation is unique and has a closed form. Analytical. Gives an analytic target for fair per-station CWs.

For mac-world: Analytic target for a proportional-fair reward (idea #14).

 Medium
2021
Distributed learning for proportional fairness (Kiefer-Wolfowitz)
Gawłowicz, Walrand, Wolisz, arXiv 2102.05149
newread
 Each node runs an asynchronous two-point gradient search on log-CW to maximise the sum of log throughputs, using throughput overheard from neighbours over 200 ms. No neural network. ns-3 with ns3-gym, 802.11n, single collision domain and flow-in-the-middle topology. About +20 % total throughput over BEB with 10 nodes. Gives the starved middle node more airtime. Using only local utility oscillates.

For mac-world: Same tools as us (ns-3 + ns3-gym), same per-node CW knob, multi-agent and decentralised. A direct, cheap baseline. Its finding that purely local utility fails matches our D1 diagnosis.

2002-06
Backoff misbehaviour games
Konorski, ToN 2006; Raya, Hubaux, Aad (DOMINO) MobiSys 2004
newknown
 Stations can cheat by using a smaller CW. Konorski models the attack as a game; DOMINO detects cheaters from observed backoff statistics. Analysis plus ns-2 and testbed. Selfish CW tuning collapses throughput unless detected or penalised.

For mac-world: Same failure mode as our independent agents (D1).

 Medium

Selfish behaviour in CSMA/CA
Čagalj, Ganeriwal, Aad, Hubaux, INFOCOM 2005
in earlier reportknown
 Non-cooperative CW game: Nash equilibria are inefficient; a repeated game with punishment (penalising strategy) restores efficiency. Analysis. Shows the price of anarchy of selfish CW choice.

For mac-world: Already in earlier report.

 High

Random access game
L. Chen, S. Low, T. Cui, J. Doyle, IEEE/ACM ToN 2010
newfound
 Reverse-engineers contention control as a game whose utility gives the CW. Designs MAC rules (gradient / best response) that converge to a chosen efficient and fair equilibrium. Analysis plus simulation. Higher throughput and better fairness than BEB with a provable equilibrium.

For mac-world: Gives a principled way to design the per-AP reward so the selfish equilibrium is efficient.

e.g. arXiv 1710.08800; Tembine et al.
newfound
 With many players, each reacts to the average behaviour of others, which keeps learning tractable as N grows. Theory. Equilibria exist and can be learned with local rules.

newread

Xin, Xu, Cai, Wang, Zhang, Liu, Guo, Luo, arXiv 2106.10307

2025
Transformer in-context learning for CSMA
Hao, Li, Duan, ACM MobiHoc 2025, arXiv 2508.09146
newread
 A one-layer transformer is given a prompt of (collision, CW threshold) examples and predicts the CW threshold for the current node density without retraining. Proves convergence and shows BEB is a special case. ns-3; trained on 2 to 6 nodes, tested on unseen densities. Within 3 % of near-optimal throughput; reaches low loss in about 100 steps where SAC needs at least 650 (summary-level figures).

For mac-world: Early RL CW paper on ns-3 with the same 15 and 50 STA counts OSCAR uses.

For mac-world: An example of a fairness reward for a single agent among unknown others, which is our independent-agent setting.

newread

drlCW for 802.11bn (per-AP MARL with neighbour observations)
Yan, Du, Zhang, Dong, IEEE VTC 2024-Spring
newread
 One DDQN agent per AP with an LSTM over 10 steps of its own and neighbours' observations, shared via beacons for APs within 40 m. Action: backoff exponent, CW = 2^(4+b). Reward is relative to the CSMA baseline: α − β·(tail delay) if throughput and loss are no worse than CSMA, otherwise −1. Single BSS and OBSS with 3 and 6 APs. More than 25 % average gain; 95th-percentile delay 15 % to 45 % lower than CSMA/CA in OBSS with no throughput loss.

For mac-world: The closest published work to mac-world: per-AP agents, multi-AP, Wi-Fi 8. Two ideas to copy: beacon-shared neighbour observations, and a reward gated on 'not worse than BEB', which directly blocks the selfish CW race.

Gaps and open questions

  • Full texts of the 2025 ICT Express MADRL framework paper, Enhanced-SETL (2024) and the "linear scaling" DQN paper could not be opened. They should be read before the final report, especially the ICT Express one, which compares agent interaction strategies like our modes.
  • No paper found combines per-AP DDPG or TD3, a partial-overlap multi-AP ns-3 topology, and a non-RL heuristic baseline. That combination is a defensible contribution for mac-world.
  • Wi-Fi 8 P-EDCA parameters differ between sources (CWmax 7 in text, 15 in one table of Liu et al.). Use the 802.11bn draft if this is cited.
  • Entries marked "known" are from background knowledge and should be checked against the original paper before they go into the final report's references.
