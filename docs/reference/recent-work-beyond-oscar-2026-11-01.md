# Is CW optimization already solved? Recent work beyond OSCAR (2023 to 2026)

Date: 2026-11-01. Builds on the 70-entry survey of 2026-10-29 (`/mnt/project-files/research/cw-optimization-survey.html`, project memory `cw-literature-survey`) and the v4 plan rev 5 (`docs/planning/mac-world-v4-plan.html` on branch `claude/project-thread-p80xpo`). It does not repeat that survey. It answers one question from the faculty review: *OSCAR is from 2023, so has someone already solved this?*

**Short answer.** No. Since 2023 the problem has become *more* active: at least ten 2024 to 2026 papers on learned or adaptive Wi-Fi contention control appeared, two of them in the last eight months, and the 802.11bn (Wi-Fi 8) standard is still a draft with final approval expected in 2028. Several recent papers state open problems that match ours almost word for word. None of the papers we checked makes or tests our v4 claim: one controller, never retrained, scored against the best fixed CW in every scenario of a multi-AP suite, never much worse than the best rival and better than each rival somewhere. Two papers come close on single parts of it, and we say so below.

Labels: **read** = page or full text fetched and read on 2026-11-01 or 2026-10-29; **abstract** = only the abstract was read; **unverified** = existence or venue not confirmed. All results are as the authors report them. None were reproduced here.

## 2. Is it "in the market" or in the standard?

- **Standard.** IEEE 802.11bn (Wi-Fi 8) is still a draft. Final approval is expected around May 2028 and Wi-Fi Alliance certification around CES 2028 ([Wi-Fi NOW, 16 June 2026](https://wifinowglobal.com/news-blog/wi-fi-8-standard-on-track-for-completion-in-1h2028-with-certification-scheduled-for-january-2028/); [Wikipedia, Wi-Fi 8](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IEEE_802.11bn), which also lists a conflicting September 2028 date). Its contention changes (high-priority EDCA, NPCA, multi-AP coordination) are new mechanisms. The standard defines the EDCA parameters an AP can advertise. How an AP picks them is left to the vendor (inference from how EDCA has worked since 802.11e; we did not find a clause that says this).
- **Products.** Juniper Mist's RRM, a commercial ML-based radio manager, lists what it adjusts: channel, transmit power, channel width, band, radio on/off and BSS colour ([Mist RRM docs](https://www.juniper.net/documentation/us/en/software/mist/mist-wireless/topics/concept/mist-radio-resource-management.html)). It does not mention EDCA, CW or backoff. We did not check every vendor, so the honest statement is: *we found no shipping product that advertises learned CW control*, not *no product does it*.

## 3. Does any paper already do our v4 claim?

Our v4 claim (plan rev 5) has four parts. A paper overlaps only if it does all four.

| Part of our claim | Who comes closest | Why it is not the same |
|---|---|---|
| **A. One controller, never retrained**, used in every scenario | Hao et al. 2025 (#5): one transformer, unseen node densities, no retraining. Nunez et al. 2025 (#8): a "general" PPO agent across deployments. | #5 covers one collision domain, varies only the node count, reports throughput only, has no multi-AP, hidden-node or fairness case. #8 is scheduling, not CW, and trains a separate agent per STA count; it calls a size-independent model "an open research direction". |
| **B. Scored against the best fixed CW in each scenario** (gap closed between BEB and the oracle) | Oh et al. 2025 (#7) has one fixed CW = 16, tuned for N = 4 only. | Nobody in the table reports a per-scenario static oracle. Most use BEB as the only baseline (#3, #6, #13) or BEB plus other learners. |
| **C. Against strong non-learned rivals** (IdleSense, PI, Gawłowicz gradient search, BLADE-like rules) as well as learned ones | None. | No paper we checked compares a learned CW controller with IdleSense or a PI controller. The 2021 negative result (Kosek-Szott et al., RL did not beat a tuned heuristic for UORA, from the earlier survey) shows why this matters. |
| **D. Multi-AP suite with fairness**, alpha-fair objective given as an input, STA-weighted fair floor | drlCW 2024 (#2): per-AP agents, 3 and 6 APs, a "no worse than CSMA" gate. Bandits 2025 (#9): several BSSs, Jain index. | drlCW trains and tests per scenario and compares only with CSMA. Its gate is the closest idea to our fair floor and must be cited as prior art for it. The bandit papers learn online per run and report that greedy learners hurt legacy BSSs by up to 59 %. Nobody conditions one policy on the fairness level alpha. |

**Honest overlaps to state up front.**
1. Per-AP or per-STA deep RL for CW is *not* new (drlCW, Cruz et al., FC-MAPPO, OSCAR itself). We must not claim it.
2. Generalising to unseen station counts without retraining is *not* new in itself (Hao et al. 2025). Our difference is the breadth of scenarios and the scoring, not the idea of generalising.
3. A "not worse than the standard" reward gate is from drlCW. Our fair floor is a stricter, STA-weighted, all-methods version of it.
4. Domain randomisation and alpha-fair utilities are standard RL and networking tools. The combination, applied to CW control and judged per cell, is what we have not found.
5. Two possibly relevant items could not be opened (#15, and a ResearchGate entry titled "Multi-Agent RL-Based Channel Access Optimization for IEEE 802.11bn", likely the same group as drlCW). Read them before the final report.

## 4. The remaining gap, with evidence

The field keeps reporting *one method beats BEB in its own setup*. What is missing is evidence that a single learned controller is *safe to deploy across many situations* when judged against the best simple alternative in each one. Evidence that this is open:

- FC-MAPPO (2025) conclusion: future work is "generalizing the method to handle more complex and dynamic scenarios, specifically addressing variations in traffic and network size".
- Nunez et al. (2025): a model that scales to arbitrary network sizes is "an open research direction"; their expert agent "degrades significantly" off its training topology.
- Casasnovas et al. (2025): multi-player greediness "may degrade coexisting networks' performance and induce policy-chasing dynamics".
- Baselines: of the 2024 to 2026 papers in the table, none compares with a per-scenario best fixed CW or with IdleSense. Our own data shows why this matters: at `20,40` IdleSense with no learning ties Global-CW OSCAR (38.77 vs 38.65 Mbps, p = 0.31), and the best static CW is 39.62. A paper that only beats BEB (27.66) would look like a +40 % win while being no better than a 2005 rule.
- Our hidden-chain result shows the trade-off a single number hides: IdleSense has the top aggregate (78.1) but leaves the middle AP at 0.53 Mbps.

## 5. Wording the student can use with faculty

> "OSCAR (2023) showed that one DDPG agent can tune one AP's contention window. The problem is not closed. Between 2024 and 2026 at least ten papers on learned Wi-Fi contention control appeared, including drlCW for Wi-Fi 8 (VTC 2024), constrained multi-agent PPO (2025), transformer in-context learning (2025) and BLADE (2026). The Wi-Fi 8 standard itself is not final until about 2028, and the commercial ML radio manager we checked tunes channel and power, not the contention window.
>
> These papers share a gap. Almost all compare only with standard backoff (BEB), in one setup, with a model trained for that setup. None checks whether its controller is ever worse than the best fixed setting or a simple adaptive rule like IdleSense, and two of them list generalisation across traffic and network size as future work. Our own measurements show why this matters: IdleSense, with no learning, ties OSCAR-style control at 20 and 40 stations.
>
> Our contribution is a robustness result, not a new 'beats BEB' number. We train one controller once and test it, without retraining, on a suite of multi-AP scenarios (one collision domain, hidden-node chains, uneven loads, several fairness levels). In each scenario we measure how much of the gap between BEB and the best fixed CW it closes, and we compare it with strong learned and non-learned rivals under the same fairness floor. We claim it is never more than 0.05 of that gap below the best rival in any scenario, and significantly better than each rival in at least one. We have not found a published paper that makes or tests this claim."

Two cautions for the defence: (1) the claim is a *plan* until M0 to M4 have run, so present it as the hypothesis being tested; (2) cite drlCW, Hao et al. and Nunez et al. as the closest work rather than waiting to be asked.
