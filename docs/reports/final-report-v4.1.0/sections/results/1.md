> **Controllers used in the answers.** The mentor's text speaks of one "global Actor" and one "global Critic". The project has no controller with exactly that shape (Part II, section 3.1), so every answer names the controller behind it. **Global-CW OSCAR** (one DDPG agent whose single output is one CW applied to every AP) is the closest to the mentor's global Actor; the **central online search** (kwdirect) is a central controller that sets a different CW per AP; **independent per-AP OSCAR** and the **PF learner** are one agent per AP. BEB, BEB+RTS/CTS, DAC, BLADE, IdleSense, KW and static CWs are the reference points. All numbers are from new simulations for this report: evaluation seeds 400 to 409, 60 s runs scored on the second half, saturated constant-rate UDP uplink, one channel, mean with 95 % confidence interval (CI) over seeds. The learners were trained for this report on 12 episodes of 15 s simulated time each, with three independent training seeds, and are evaluated frozen.

### Answers to the research questions

1. **Can the global Actor learn appropriate CW values for both APs?** Yes, in the sense of reaching the best common CW. Global-CW OSCAR improves on BEB by +15.3 % (1A), +23.9 % (1B), +27.0 % (1C) and +34.3 % (1D). The best common static CW, found by search on separate seeds, gives +15.4 %, +24.2 %, +31.9 % and +36.8 %, so the learner is within 0.1 and 0.4 points of it in 1A and 1B and within 5.0 and 2.5 points in 1C and 1D. Its CW is the same for both APs by construction (mean 139 slots in 1A, 485 in 1D). In these four symmetric cells the best answer is a symmetric one, so no controller is expected to learn two different values; the per-AP controllers (central online search, PF learner) end with similar values for the two APs (1A: 96 and 95 slots).
2. **Does joint CW optimization outperform applying the same CW to both APs?** Not in symmetric cells. The best common static CW (the "same CW for both" case) is, within the confidence intervals, as good as or better than every controller that can set a different CW per AP: central online search +11.9 % to +35.4 % against +15.4 % to +36.8 % for the common CW (1A to 1D). Where the two APs differ, in the unequal-station and hidden-node cells, the picture changes (scenarios 2, 4 and 7).
3. **Does the approach reduce collisions compared with independent per-AP CW optimization?** Yes. The PHY loss (share of transmission attempts that fail) is 0.56 for Global-CW OSCAR against 0.80 for independent per-AP OSCAR in 1A, and 0.56 against 0.78 in 1D. Independent per-AP OSCAR ends below BEB in goodput (-33.6 %, -35.0 %, -46.1 %, -16.2 % in 1A to 1D) and unevenly split between the two APs (1A: 10.0 Mbit/s against 13.1).
4. **Does it improve inter-BSS fairness?** It keeps the BSSs equal, but BEB already does that in a symmetric cell: Jain's index over stations is 0.99 to 1.00 for BEB and for Global-CW OSCAR in all four cells. The improvement is against independent per-AP OSCAR (Jain 0.98 in 1A and 0.94 in 1D, worst station 1.00 and 0.37 Mbit/s against 1.98 and 0.74 for Global-CW OSCAR).
