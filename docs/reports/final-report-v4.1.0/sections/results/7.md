### Answers (the mentor's five measures)

- **Collision probability.** BEB's PHY loss ratio is 0.65 in 2C and 0.62 in S4 against 0.66 and 0.68 in the one-domain cells 1A and 3A: the hidden pair does not raise the loss ratio of the whole network, it changes who bears it (the middle BSS, below). The controllers that raise throughput lower it: in 2C DAC 0.55, Global-CW OSCAR 0.57, the central online search (alpha 1) 0.56, the PF learner 0.53.
- **Packet loss and retries.** The mean per-AP retry rate (share of transmissions that are retries) is lowest with RTS/CTS (0.15 in 2C, against 0.40 for BEB); this does not translate into goodput (see the RTS/CTS point).
- **Throughput.** In the chain BEB gives the middle BSS 1.1 Mbit/s (S4: 1.2) while the outer BSSs get 34.1. Global CW optimisation (one CW for all APs) raises the total (Global-CW OSCAR +9.5 % in 2C) but leaves the middle BSS at 2.6 Mbit/s; per-AP CWs raise the middle BSS to 8.3 Mbit/s (central online search) or 6.7 (PF learner) and cost total goodput (-4.8 % and -5.8 %). No controller raises the middle BSS without lowering the total here. The central online search gives the middle BSS the most (8.3 Mbit/s); the common static CW chosen for alpha 1 gives it 6.3 Mbit/s at -0.6 % total, and the one chosen for alpha 0 gives 2.9 Mbit/s at +10.0 %. So a per-AP CW buys a larger middle share, not a better trade between total and middle share.
- **Delay.** In these saturated cells the delay is dominated by the queue (4.7 to 5.4 s mean at the outer BSSs in 2C). The visible effect is on the middle BSS: BEB's 99th-percentile delay is 8.8 s there against 6.9 s at AP1; every other controller brings it down to between 5.7 and 6.6 s, i.e. closer to the outer BSSs.
- **Impact of RTS/CTS.** RTS/CTS lowers total goodput by 30 % to 31 % in all six cells measured, hidden or not (table above). In the hidden chain it also lowers the middle BSS (2C: 1.10 to 0.45 Mbit/s; S4: 1.25 to 0.50 Mbit/s) and raises its delay. RTS/CTS therefore does not fix this hidden-node topology in this simulator; the mechanism was not isolated (unknown). This agrees with the project's earlier measurements (Part II, section 5.4).
