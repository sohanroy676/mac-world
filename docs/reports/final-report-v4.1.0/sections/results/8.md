### Result for this scenario

**Status: not supported by the simulator in version v4.1.0; no experiment was run, and none is reported.**

- **What the simulator does.** Every BSS carries constant-rate UDP traffic in the uplink direction only (stations send, the AP receives). Goodput is counted at the AP. The AP's own channel access is used for acknowledgements only, and the CW controller writes the CW of the stations of each BSS.
- **Why that blocks this scenario.** The scenario asks how a controller copes with competition between AP transmissions and station transmissions. With uplink-only traffic the AP never competes for data frames, so downlink and bidirectional BSSs cannot be built, and any number printed for them would not be a measurement of the scenario.
- **What is needed.** A downlink sender on every AP with receivers on the stations, goodput counted at the stations, a per-AP direction flag (uplink, downlink, both), the AP's own CW under the controller, and new reference optima for these cells. The estimate is one to two days of implementation and validation and about 150 runs (about 60 lab-hours) for three or four APs and five methods.
- **Plan.** This work is the first item of the post-report list (`docs/planning/post-report-todo.md`) and is on the roadmap; it was deferred until after this report.
- **What can already be said.** Scenario 6 (heterogeneous loads, below) shows how the controllers behave when BSSs differ in demand, which is the closest available evidence; it does not stand in for the uplink/downlink mix.
