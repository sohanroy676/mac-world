### Answers to the research questions

1. **Does the controller adapt CW according to station density?** The controllers that gain the most goodput do not, and they do not need to. In 4B to 4D the common static CW gives +59.7 %, +53.8 % and +49.8 % over BEB, Global-CW OSCAR (one CW for all) +57.1 %, +50.9 % and +44.8 %, and the central online search, which can set a CW per AP, ends with nearly the same CW for every AP (4B: 579 / 609 / 593 / 506 slots for the APs with 10, 20, 30, 40 stations). In a single collision domain a common CW gives every station the same chance to transmit, so the per-station share is already fair and the total goodput is already near the best. The learners that do set different CWs per AP raise the CW with the AP's station count (4B, PF learner: 433 / 784 / 717 / 875 slots; independent per-AP OSCAR: 16 / 16 / 433 / 984), and neither improves on the common CW: the PF learner gains +55.4 % in 4B, independent per-AP OSCAR loses 71.3 %, and both lower the per-station fairness (below).
2. **Does it improve fairness between heavily and lightly loaded BSSs?** Measured per station, yes against BEB: the worst station gets 0.19 Mbit/s under BEB in 4B and 0.35 with the central online search, 0.30 with Global-CW OSCAR, 0.33 with the common static CW; Jain's index over stations is 0.96 (BEB), 0.99, 0.97, 0.98. Floor test (each BSS keeps at least the smaller of its BEB goodput and its proportional share; passed in 100 % of seeds by Global-CW OSCAR and 30 % by BEB in 4B). The PF learner and independent per-AP OSCAR break the per-station balance (Jain 0.77 and 0.54 in 4B, 0.58 and 0.54 in 4C).
3. **Does it prevent one AP from dominating channel access?** Yes for every controller that uses a common or searched CW. The clear failure is independent per-AP OSCAR: the worst station gets 0.01 Mbit/s in 4B (BEB 0.19) and its total goodput is -71.3 % against BEB, because the agents of the two smaller BSSs learn the smallest CW (16 slots) while the agent of the 40-station BSS ends at 984, so the largest BSS is starved.

Aggregate goodput alone would hide this, as the mentor notes: total goodput of the PF learner in 4B (+55.4 %) is close to the best, while its per-station fairness (Jain 0.77) is lower than that of BEB (0.96), Global-CW OSCAR (0.97) and the central online search (0.99).
