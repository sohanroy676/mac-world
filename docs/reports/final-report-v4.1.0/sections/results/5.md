### Answers (the five measures of the mentor's text)

- **Convergence after topology changes and time to stabilise CW.** CW settling time (seconds until the mean CW stays within 10 % of its level at the end of the phase): Global-CW OSCAR 0.0 s, PF learner 2.0 s, central online search 0.6 s, KW 3.6 s, DAC 6.0 s. A frozen learner reacts on the next 10 ms step, so its CW settles at once; DAC and KW move their CW over the whole run (CW panel).
- **Throughput degradation during transitions.** When stations join, the lowest second in the first five seconds compared with the previous phase's level is: Global-CW OSCAR 7.1 %, DAC -0.8 %, central online search -4.6 %, BEB -7.0 %, KW -13.7 % (a positive value means the goodput rose). When stations leave there is a large drop for every controller, BEB included (BEB -21.5 %, DAC -34.2 %, Global-CW OSCAR -34.5 %), so this drop comes from the scenario mechanics (stations leaving the network) and not from CW adaptation; its exact cause was not isolated in this study (unknown).
- **Collision probability.** Measured as the retry rate: lowest for Global-CW OSCAR (0.09), then BLADE (0.11) and the static proportional CW (0.16); BEB 0.60; the independent per-AP agents 0.53.
- **Recovery time.** Time until goodput stays within 5 % of the phase level: 2.0 s for Global-CW OSCAR, 2.0 s for BEB, DAC and BLADE, 2.6 s for the central online search, 13.0 s for KW.
- **Which controller adapts best overall?** By mean goodput over the 120 s: DAC (37.8 Mbit/s), then the best common static CW (alpha 0) (37.3), the proportional static CW (37.3). Global-CW OSCAR reaches 33.3 Mbit/s, BEB 28.8, BEB+RTS/CTS 20.5 and independent per-AP OSCAR 22.7. The two best are not learners: an online rule (DAC) and a static CW chosen in advance (on separate seeds, at the full 25 stations per AP) do as well as anything that adapts, which suggests that in this single-domain cell the best CW is nearly flat between 10 and 25 stations per AP (inference).
