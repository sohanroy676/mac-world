### Answers to the research questions

- **Can the Actor allocate channel access opportunities according to traffic demand?** Under saturation (6S) every AP's offered load exceeds its fair share, so the demand-proportional allocation of the mentor's example is not feasible; what the best controllers do is give every station the same share (per AP goodput 9.9 Mbit/s for the common static CW; 9.7 for Global-CW OSCAR), which is the fair outcome, and BEB leaves AP1 and AP4 behind (7.3 / 6.9 Mbit/s). Under 6U, where all the demand fits, the allocation is demand-proportional by construction and the question becomes whether every AP gets what it asks for: BEB, DAC, IdleSense, KW, the static CWs, Global-CW OSCAR and the PF learner carry 2.50 / 5.00 / 7.50 / 10.00 Mbit/s, i.e. all of it.
- **Does the optimization improve delay under heterogeneous loads?** Yes, against BEB, for the controllers that tune to the load. In 6U the mean MAC delay of Global-CW OSCAR is 6.6 / 4.9 / 3.8 / 3.8 ms at AP1 to AP4 against 11.5 / 7.5 / 6.0 / 5.5 ms for BEB (reductions of 42 %, 35 %, 37 % and 32 %), and the 99th percentile 16.2 / 14.9 / 14.3 / 13.7 ms against 31.7 / 28.3 / 24.3 / 22.9 ms. IdleSense and the central online search (alpha 0) are about equal to Global-CW OSCAR; the static CW chosen for saturation (11.0 / 9.3 / 9.0 / 12.7 ms mean) is no better than BEB (probably because it is too large for a lightly loaded channel; inference). BEB+RTS/CTS multiplies the delay (79.3 / 139.8 / 198.8 / 329.7 ms at AP1 to AP4) and loses AP4's traffic (7.1 of 10 Mbit/s delivered). In 6S all controllers show 5.2 to 5.6 s of delay: the delay is the time in a full queue and does not measure the controller.
- **Does it avoid starving low-load APs?** Yes for all controllers except independent per-AP OSCAR in 6S (total -67.3 % against BEB, 2.5 Mbit/s per AP, worst station 0.22 Mbit/s; PHY loss 0.94). In 6U the lowest-load AP (AP1) keeps at least 2.49 of its 2.5 Mbit/s under every controller except BLADE (2.29 Mbit/s), independent per-AP OSCAR (2.41 Mbit/s) and BEB+RTS/CTS (2.47 Mbit/s).

**Status:** answered for constant-rate UDP, saturated and unsaturated; bursty and mixed UDP/TCP traffic not run.
