### Results

How the topologies were built: the simulator has a yes/no switch for pairs of BSSs that cannot hear each other (`--apHidden`). 2A is "AP3 hidden from AP1 and AP2" (AP1 and AP2 overlap; AP3 is isolated), 2B is "AP1 hidden from AP2 and AP3", and 2C is the chain (AP1 and AP3 hidden from each other, AP2 hears both). Stations per AP are 10, 10, 10 in 2A and 2B and 15, 15, 15 in 2C, as in the mentor's table. **Experiment 2D (different interference strengths) was not run**: the simulator has only audible or deaf pairs, no graded strengths (the plan to add them is item 2 of the post-report list).

### Answers

- **Impact of the interference relationships on the optimal CW.** In 2A and 2B the isolated AP does not interact with the pair, and the pair behaves like the two-AP cell of scenario 1: the best controllers (DAC, KW, the PF learner, the common static CW, the central online search) improve on BEB by about +10 % to +11 % of the total (the isolated AP's goodput is part of the total, which dilutes the percentage compared with 1A). CWs differ between APs only mildly: in 2A the central online search ends with 110 / 109 / 81 slots for AP1, AP2, AP3, and in 2B with 86 / 122 / 120, so the isolated AP uses a smaller CW than the two that compete, as the mentor expected.
- **Global-CW OSCAR is sensitive to which AP is isolated.** One CW for all APs gives +7.8 % in 2A but -0.9 % in 2B: the single value it learns (244 slots in 2B) is probably wrong for the isolated AP (inference). The PF learner, which has one CW per AP, gives +10.6 % and +8.8 %. This is an effect of the controller shape (one CW for all APs), not of the topology alone.
- **The chain (2C) is the case where interference changes the optimum.** BEB gives the middle BSS 1.1 Mbit/s in total while the outer BSSs get 34.1 and 34.2: the worst station gets 0.07 Mbit/s and Jain's index over stations is 0.69. Controllers that raise the middle BSS's share do so at the cost of total goodput: the central online search gives the middle BSS 8.3 Mbit/s (outer 28.7 and 29.0) with Jain 0.84, at -4.7 % total goodput against BEB; the PF learner 6.7 Mbit/s in the middle (Jain 0.81, -5.9 %). Controllers that maximise total goodput (DAC +11.7 %, Global-CW OSCAR +9.4 %) leave the middle BSS at 1.4 and 2.6 Mbit/s. KW and the fair learners trade throughput for fairness, the others do the opposite. A common CW cannot do both (the common static CW chosen for alpha 1: -0.7 % total, middle BSS 6.3 Mbit/s; the one chosen for alpha 0 gives the opposite trade).
- **Interference matrix in the state (the mentor's remark).** In this project a coarse BSS-level loss matrix builds the topology (which pairs of BSSs hear each other; ADR-0005), but it is not an input to any controller compared here: every controller learns from measured goodput, loss or idle time. The 2C result shows what such an input could be for: a controller that knows the chain could set the middle BSS's CW apart at once, while the controllers here need measurement time to find it (the central online search does find a per-AP CW with Jain 0.84, above). Giving the matrix to a controller as an input is not implemented; it is listed with the graded-interference work (post-report list, item 2).

**Status:** answered for 2A, 2B and 2C; 2D not run (no graded interference in the simulator).
