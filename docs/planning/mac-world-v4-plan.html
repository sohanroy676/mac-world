mac-world v4 plan: one controller that is near-optimal in every scenario

The final plan for the rest of the project. It is rebuilt on the v3.3.0 to v3.4.2 results that landed after revision 3, and every assumption was checked against the code on dev 33708ff. It replaces revisions 1 to 3.

Bottom line

  1. Where every AP hears every other, a 2005 heuristic already matches OSCAR. At 20,40, IdleSense with the published rule and no learning gives 38.77 ± 0.21 Mbps against Global-CW OSCAR's 38.65 ± 0.40 (n=8, p = 0.31). That is +40 % over BEB and close to the best static CW of 39.62 fact. No learner can claim a real aggregate win here.
  2. In the flow-in-the-middle chain, every baseline fails on one side. IdleSense has the highest aggregate (78.12) but starves the middle AP to 0.53 Mbps. Global-CW leaves it at 2.6 Mbps. Static per-AP CWs are fair only after a per-scenario search. A PF-reward learner reaches the static oracle's PF, but how close it gets depends on the training seed fact.
  3. So the contribution is robustness across scenarios, not one more win in one of them. The headline metric is the share of the BEB-to-oracle gap each method closes in every scenario and at every fairness level. The claim: one trained controller, never retrained, is never meaningfully worse than the best baseline in any cell, and beats each baseline clearly in at least one. IdleSense, Global-CW, OSCAR and the static settings each have a scenario where they collapse inference, partly fact.
  4. The method is simplified to the parts that survived testing (working name RAFA): one shared DDPG actor fed by the AP's own idle-slot, retry and load readings; a fairness reward weighted by station count, with the fairness level as an input; a fair floor against BEB; training over randomised scenarios; and a 3-seed ensemble at deployment. The analytic-prior residual, TD3 and the backoff-mode action are dropped, because v3.3 and v3.4 showed no stable benefit from them fact.
  5. The largest risk is variance between training seeds, not compute. One-seed rankings flipped three times in v3.4.0 fact. So every comparison uses 3 training seeds, and the deployed controller is their ensemble.

The claim and how it is scored

METRIC

Gap closed (GC)

GC = (U_α − U_α,BEB) / (U_α,oracle − U_α,BEB) per scenario and α ∈ {0, 1, 2}. BEB scores 0 and the per-cell static oracle scores 1; a method that starves an AP can go below 0. The oracle is picked on selection seeds 0 to 2 from one static grid, so a single grid serves every α.

CLAIM 1

Never meaningfully worse

In every cell of the core suite, RAFA's GC is not below the best baseline's by more than 0.05 (one-sided test, margin fixed now).

CLAIM 2

Every rival beaten somewhere

For each baseline there is at least one cell where RAFA's GC is significantly higher (Holm across baselines within the cell).

HEADLINE

Best mean and worst case

RAFA has the highest mean GC and the highest worst-cell GC across the suite. Both are reported for every method in one table.

This makes "beats all existing methods" concrete and honest. RAFA does not need to win the one-domain cells where IdleSense ties the ceiling. It needs to be close there and clearly better where each rival breaks: IdleSense and Global-CW in the chain, static settings and per-scenario learners in dynamic and unseen scenarios, BEB everywhere. Aggregate goodput, per-AP throughput, Jain, delay and collision rate are still reported for every cell.

Statistics

  • Seeds: selection python seeds 0 to 2 for tuning and oracle choice; evaluation python seeds 100 to 109 (ns-3 101 to 110), reusing the v3.4.0 and v3.3.1 runs on 100 to 107; training seeds disjoint from both.
  • Training seeds: 3 for RAFA (and its ensemble) and for the PF learner and drlCW; 1 for OSCAR, CCOD and UCB, which are not the main rivals. A RAFA claim also needs each of its 3 seeds to point the same way.
  • Two stages: Stage 1 runs every method on seeds 100 to 104 and ranks the baselines per cell, reported in full. Stage 2 adds seeds 105 to 109 for RAFA and the top 4 baselines in each cell, so each cell tests at most 4 rivals at n=10.
  • Tests: paired t on per-seed GC differences (primary) and exact Wilcoxon (reported). Holm within each cell: first threshold 0.05/4 = 0.0125. With n=10, 10 of 10 or 9 of 10 seed wins can reach it; with n=8 the exact Wilcoxon minimum of 0.0078 would not survive many comparisons, which is why revision 2 moved to n=10. Claim 1 is a one-sided non-inferiority test with margin 0.05 GC.
  • Report: the GC table (method × cell, mean and worst), wins / ties / losses, Pareto plots of aggregate against min-AP for S4 to S6, delay CDFs for S7, and time series for S8.

Decisions taken

You delegated these. Each is recorded here and in the repo and can be reversed.

  1. Claim: robustness across the suite (GC, Claims 1 and 2), not a win in one scenario. The physics leave no other honest big claim: in one domain IdleSense already ties the ceiling.
  2. Method: RAFA as above. Residual prior, TD3 and the backoff-mode action are dropped on the v3.3 and v3.4 evidence. DDPG stays.
  3. Fairness: station-weighted α-fair utility and a station-weighted fair floor.
  4. Variance: 3 training seeds everywhere that matters, ensemble deployment, n=10 evaluation seeds.
  5. Scope: per-STA on/off traffic yes (ADR-0006). Shared actor with team reward as the main method (ADR-0007). Neighbour summaries only as the RAFA-N variant.
  6. Compute: current machine, lanes fixed first, lower-load test, trim order.
  7. Baselines: re-implemented ones are labelled as such and built only from texts that were read.
