Plan: v2.0.0 section of `docs/detailed_roadmap.md`. Tables/figures: `docs/figures/v2.0.0/<experiment>/<topology>/` (generated by `aggregate_bench.py`). Raw run dirs are local only (`results/`, not committed, user decision).

## A. Fixed-total-STA sweep (60 STAs)

### A.1 Setup (fact)
- Purpose: separate AP count from load. In v1.6.0 the total STA count changed with N (60/50/105/180/220), so the two were confounded (`docs/scaling-v1.6.0.md`).
- Topologies: 30,30 (N=2) · 15 x 4 (N=4) · 10 x 6 (N=6) · 6 x 10 (N=10). Total 60 STAs in every case, equal split.
- Modes: BEB, global-CW OSCAR, independent per-AP. Seeds 0-2. Default config (simTime 15, step 0.01, 1500 steps, 150 Mbps per STA). Policy window = steps >= 300. n=3 per cell.
- 36/36 runs PASS `--check`. One ns-3 job at a time. Run times 902-1340 s, no trend with N at fixed STAs.
- Command: `benchmark_v1_1_0.py --topos 30,30 15,15,15,15 10,10,10,10,10,10 6,6,6,6,6,6,6,6,6,6 --benchDir results/bench-v2.0.0-fixed60`.
- Code under test: branch `feature/v2.0.0-robustness` (adds `--offeredLoad`, default 150, so behavior at default is the same as v1.x).

### A.3 Observations (fact unless marked)
- **BEB is flat at fixed load:** 27.38-28.06 Mbps from N=2 to N=10. The BEB decline in v1.6.0 (27.96 to 19.24 Mbps, N=2 to 10) therefore came with the growth in total STAs (60 to 220), not with AP count alone. Both varied together there; this run changes only the AP count.
- **Global is above BEB at every N:** +30.7 to +36.4 %, paired CI excludes 0 at all four N, and the lowest global seed (34.45) is above the highest BEB mean (28.06). Global is also flat in N (36.46-37.34). Phy loss is lower than BEB (about 0.60 vs 0.73).
- **Independent falls as AP count grows at constant load:** about equal to BEB at N=2 (+0.9 ± 69.7 %), then 17.69, 19.79 and 16.70 Mbps at N=4, 6, 10. The paired CI excludes 0 at N=4 and N=10 (negative) but not at N=6 or N=2. Below the BEB cell mean in 8 of 9 seeds at N>=4 (the exception is N=6 seed 1, 29.68).
- **Fairness (independent):** Jain falls from 0.972 (N=2) to 0.607 (N=10), while BEB and global stay at or above 0.956. Higher Jain with lower throughput would not be an improvement, but here both fall.
- **Seed spread (independent):** N=2 ranges 19.77-35.00 and N=6 ranges 14.46-29.68, so it is unstable at small n. N=10 is uniformly low (15.95-17.53).

### A.4 Inference and limits (not tested)
- Inference: independent per-AP agents get worse with AP count even when load is constant. This supports "AP count drives the independent-agent decline", but n=3 and the N=2/N=6 CIs include 0.
- Not tested: why. Candidates (none run): more simultaneous learners making each AP's environment non-stationary, per-AP reward noise as STAs per AP shrink (6 STAs at N=10), and the shared warm-up. These would need separate experiments.
- Equal split only; unequal splits are Batch B. Single load (150 Mbps per STA, saturated); lower loads are Batch C.
- n=3: "CI includes 0" does not show equality.
- Association/deassociation behavior at these topologies was not analysed here (deferred to Batch D). The dry association smoke at 6 x 10 showed 6/6 on every AP.

### A.5 Verdict (Batch A)
- Global-CW generalizes across AP count at fixed load (positive and CI-supported at N=2, 4, 6, 10).
- Independent per-AP does not: it matches BEB only at N=2 and is below it at N>=4.
- The AP-count effect is not an artifact of total STA load (the v1.6.0 confound is resolved in this direction for BEB and independent).

## B. AP imbalance (60 total STAs)

### B.1 Setup (fact)
- Purpose: one AP heavily loaded, the others light. Offered load per AP scales with its STA count (150 Mbps per STA).
- Topologies: 5,55 (N=2) and 5,5,5,45 (N=4). Total 60 STAs, the same as Batch A, so the balanced references are 30,30 and 15 x 4 (A.2). The v1.1.0 20,40 anchor (n=5) is a mild imbalance.
- Modes BEB / global / independent, seeds 0-2, default config, n=3 per cell. 18/18 runs PASS `--check`. Run times 865-1976 s (the slowest, 1976 s, was 5,55 global seed 1; the machine was not otherwise loaded).
- Command: `benchmark_v1_1_0.py --topos 5,55 5,5,5,45 --benchDir results/bench-v2.0.0-imbalance`. Figures: `docs/figures/v2.0.0/imbalance/`.

### B.3 Observations (fact unless marked)
- **BEB aggregate is insensitive to imbalance:** 28.64 and 27.25 Mbps, in line with the balanced values (27.4-27.6). BEB's Jain falls (0.62, 0.51) because the large AP carries most of the load; Jain here is computed on raw per-AP throughput, not demand-normalized.
- **Global keeps its gain:** +27.1 % (CI includes 0, n=3, all three seeds above the BEB mean) at 5,55 and +31.9 % (CI excludes 0) at 5,5,5,45, similar to the balanced +34 to +36 %. The gain goes to the big AP (AP2 25.4 to 33.7; AP4 18.5 to 28.0). Small APs do not gain (AP1 3.20 to 2.62 at 5,55). One shared CW cannot treat APs differently, and Jain falls to 0.58 and 0.40.
- **Independent is not clearly different from BEB in aggregate:** -15.2 ± 87.1 % and -4.3 ± 24.1 %, both CIs include 0. The 5,55 spread across seeds is large (14.30 to 34.63).
- **Independent redistributes:** it gives small APs more (5,55: AP1 3.20 to 7.68; 5,5,5,45: the three small APs from 2.8-3.1 to 4.7-8.1) and the big AP less (AP2 25.44 to 16.68; AP4 18.49 to 7.47). Its Jain (0.845, 0.878) is the highest of the three modes. Inference: this higher Jain mostly comes from lowering the big AP, because it is a raw-throughput Jain and the aggregate does not rise. It should not be read as a fairness gain.
- **Independent vs balanced:** at N=4, independent is -4.3 ± 24.1 % under imbalance (5,5,5,45) against -35.7 ± 30.1 % when balanced (15 x 4, A.2). The CIs overlap and I did not test the difference, so this is only an observation.
- **20,40 anchor (v1.1.0, n=5):** BEB 27.96 ± 1.26, global 36.89 ± 3.62 (+31.9 ± 11.8 %), independent 25.27 ± 8.21 (-9.3 ± 31.0 %). Same picture as 5,55 and 5,5,5,45.

### B.4 Inference and limits (not tested)
- Not tested: why independent loses less under imbalance. One idea is that fewer STAs per light AP make its own reward easier to learn, but that is a guess.
- Reward is per-AP normalized (own goodput / own offered load), so a light AP can get a high reward at low throughput. No per-AP reward analysis was done here.
- One heavy AP only; 2 and 4 APs only; single load level; n=3.

### B.5 Verdict (Batch B)
- Global-CW still gains about +27 to +32 % under imbalance (CI-supported at 5,5,5,45), but only the heavy AP benefits.
- Independent per-AP shows no CI-supported aggregate difference from BEB under imbalance. It trades big-AP throughput for small-AP throughput, which raises Jain without raising the aggregate.
